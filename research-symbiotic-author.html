The Symbiotic Author as an Institutional Category

Authorship, Creative Provenance, and Eligibility in Human–AI Literary Production

Navi Musaget · Preprint v0.5 · September 2026 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23047241
Open the research record → FFRI-NM research index →
The category

What do we call the human author when AI materially changes the work?

This preprint proposes the Symbiotic Author as a work-relative institutional category for cases in which an identifiable human author participates in a materially consequential, bidirectionally conditioned creative relation with a generative system.

The category does not depend on how much machine-generated language survives in the final artifact. A work may contain no surviving machine-generated prose while its development has been materially shaped through sustained human–system interaction. Conversely, substantial machine-generated prose may arise through one-directional generation without a qualifying symbiotic relation.

The relation may be symbiotic without the authorship being shared.
Creative provenance

Text origin and creative causation are different questions.

The framework distinguishes textual provenance from creative causal provenance.

Textual provenance asks where surviving language or other material originated. Creative causal provenance asks which interactions materially changed the trajectory of the work.

This distinction makes rejected system contributions analytically visible. A generated proposal may leave no textual trace while still causing a consequential human reinterpretation, refusal, structural change or new creative direction.

What machine-generated material survives in the artifact?

Institutional architecture

Classification is not eligibility.

The paper separates four questions that are often collapsed in debates over AI authorship:

1
What author–system relation occurred?
2
What machine-generated material survives?
3
What evidence supports the process account?
4
What does the institution permit?

On this architecture, authorship classification, disclosure, creative provenance, evidentiary corroboration and institutional eligibility remain analytically distinct.

An institution may therefore recognize a work as symbiotically authored while permitting it, restricting it, requiring disclosure, prohibiting retained machine-generated prose, or excluding the work entirely under its own policy.

The framework is designed to improve the precision of institutional rules, not to predetermine whether those rules should be permissive or restrictive.
Where the claim stops

A human institutional category, not machine personhood.

The Symbiotic Author framework does not treat generative systems as human-equivalent co-authors and does not require claims about machine consciousness, intention, personhood or moral agency.

It does not determine copyrightability, originality, literary quality, plagiarism, ethical legitimacy or institutional admission.

Its narrower purpose is to give institutions a more precise object of classification when materially consequential human–AI interaction becomes part of a work's creative history without displacing the human authorial locus.
