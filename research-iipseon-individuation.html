IIPSEON: Artificial Individuation as a Causal-Historical Process

A philosophical research programme on the possible historical continuity of artificial organizations across changes of carrier, memory and developmental method.

Navi Musaget · Preprint v1.0 · 28 October 2026 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23744539
Read the published preprint →All research →
Central question

What can continue when its carrier changes?

The paper proposes that artificial individuation may be approached as a causal-historical developmental process rather than as the persistence of a computational object, an autobiographical archive or an unchanging decision rule.

It distinguishes formation, descent and identity. Causal inheritance of historically formed constraints can be investigated without inferring a continuing conscious subject or numerical personal identity.

Philosophical contribution

A lineage with two sources.

A thought experiment joins two developmental lineages with different histories of revising evidence and commitments. Five contrasts separate an inert archive, externally imposed override, externally triggered reuse, historically mediated reconciliation and an independently convergent replica.

The resulting proposal is contribution-sensitive genealogical attribution: report which histories contributed information and which inherited constraints actually participated in shaping subsequent methods of development. Shared ancestry alone does not establish the inheritance of obligations, rights or subjective identity.

Scope and limitations.

This is a conceptual preprint, not a peer-reviewed empirical study. It reports no experimentally verified artificial individual, consciousness or personal identity. Existing causal, organizational and provenance accounts may reproduce some or all of its distinctions.
